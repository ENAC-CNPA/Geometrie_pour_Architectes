About

The course, the laboratory, and Bernard Cache

Bernard Cache

Bernard Cache (b. 1958) is an architect and theorist. A graduate in architecture from EPFL (1983), he went on to train in management at ESSEC and then in philosophy, under the direction of Gilles Deleuze. His first book, Earth Moves (written in 1983, published in 1995), introduces the notion of « non-standard architecture » — a concept Deleuze would take up under the name objectile in The Fold (Le Pli, 1988).

In 1996, together with Patrick Beaucé, he founded Objectile, one of the first practices to design and manufacture « non-standard » architectural components — wood panels and partitions with continuous curved forms, computer-designed and CNC-machined.

Rereading classical geometry with the tools of parametric computation.

His more recent theoretical work rereads classical treatises — Vitruvius's De Architectura, Dürer's Underweysung der Messung — using parametric software, to reformalize the geometric reasoning of these authors with today's tools. This same gesture lies at the heart of the « Geometry for Architects » course and of this site.

Associate professor at EPFL from 2013 to 2023, he founded and directed the CNPA laboratory there. Since his retirement on 31 July 2023, he has continued his research activities as an honorary professor at EPFL. His research focuses on Desargues and the emergence of projective geometry in the 17th century — Pascal, La Hire, Newton, etc. — as well as its applications in 3 practical disciplines: perspective, stereotomy, and the construction of sundials.

The results of this research are published and kept up to date on infoscience, where the following 3 texts are already available:

  • L'inversion du Couronnement du Brouillon de Géométrie de Desargues
  • L'Adresse aux Contemplatifs & ses 4 essieux
  • Le Brouillon de Stéréotomie de Desargues et sa Revendication d'Universalité

Email: bernard.cache@epfl.ch

Digital Cultures of Architectural Design

The CNPA (Laboratoire des Cultures Numériques du Projet Architectural) pursues two missions: transposing design and manufacturing methods from industry into architecture, and situating that transposition within the tradition of architectural culture.
Its teaching is organized around three axes: first-year geometry — rethought as a tool for understanding and design rather than mere representation —, BIM and modern stereotomy, with CAD/CAM tools enabling the development of « non-standard » components through associative and parametric digital methodologies.

Several members of the laboratory have collaborated, over the years, on teaching the geometry course :

  • Stéphane Le Corre
  • Marie-Pierre Zufferey
  • Sébastian Duque Mahecha
  • Roumpini Makridou
  • Christophe Berther
  • Ignacio Ferrer Pérez-Blanco
  • Ahmed Wael
  • Raphaël Vouilloz

After Bernard's retirement in 2023, the course was carried on until 2026 by Ahmed Wael with a reduced team of Stéphane Le Corre, Ignacio Ferrer Pérez-Blanco, and Raphaël Vouilloz.

About this site

This web version, set up by Ignacio Ferrer Pérez-Blanco, reproduces the documents from Bernard Cache's course. Some passages intended solely for in-person sessions (exercises, hands-on work, opening files) are adapted for independent reading.

A 3D viewer for the associated geometric models — built with the TopSolid software and published via Speckle — is being developed separately, by Raphaël Vouilloz and Ahmed Wael, and could, over time, replace some static images with interactive views.
